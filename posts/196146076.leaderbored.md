*Is it a race or a run?*

Yes, sure, this cuts a lot of corners. Good AI isn’t just built by computers; it’s also built by good researchers, unique training data, and, increasingly, [the product around the AI](https://cloudedjudgement.substack.com/p/clouded-judgement-41026-long-live). AI companies can do many things at once; their computers are not so easily fungible; many of their computers are actually, [famously](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2025/10/31/technology/openai-fundraising-deals.html), also [their](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/23/sundar-pichai-is-very-excited-about-google-clouds-openai-partnership/) competitors’ [computers](https://www.anthropic.com/news/google-broadcom-partnership-compute), leased [back](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-amazon-compute) and [forth](https://openai.com/index/amazon-partnership/) in a giant trillion-dollar web of incomprehensible partnerships and frenemy-ships. There is no knob that says “build” on one side and “sell” on the other.

Still, that *is* the pattern, isn’t it? In 2023 and 2024, OpenAI was the dominant default, Anthropic was the IYKYK upstart, and Google was incinerating its reputation, [and many billions of dollars](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/09/1155650909/google-chatbot--error-bard-shares), one disastrous demo at a time. Then, Google built the best new models—“Gemini 2.5 is the [new SoTA](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/gemini-25-is-the-new-sota);” “I tested Google’s new Nano Banana image AI, and [it’s insane](https://medium.com/the-generator/i-tested-googles-new-nano-banana-image-ai-and-it-s-insane-826d23f7f9a7)”—and became the smart money. Then, Anthropic put out Opus 4.5; it was a “[world-changing shift](https://every.to/source-code/how-every-is-harnessing-the-world-changing-shift-of-opus-4-5);” everyone became obsessed with Claude Code; “[move over, ChatGPT.](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/01/claude-code-ai-hype/685617/)” Anthropic blew past OpenAI’s hype, [its revenue](https://x.com/aakashgupta/status/2041283873108332878), and [its valuation](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-beats-openai-secondary-markets-213828157.html); OpenAI started [missing revenue targets](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openai-misses-key-revenue-user-targets-in-high-stakes-sprint-toward-ipo-94a95273). But revenue is a lagging indicator. Over the last six months, while Anthropic was selling Opus and Claude Code—and [struggling](https://x.com/bcherny/status/2040213608064491525) to find [enough computers](https://x.com/patrick_oshag/status/2047344831253315886) to do so—OpenAI was building GPT 5.5, out last week. And GPT 5.5 [has it all](https://every.to/vibe-check/gpt-5-5); GPT 5.5 [blew people away](https://natesnewsletter.substack.com/p/chatgpt-55-scored-87-where-the-next); GPT 5.5 is [a new frontier in everything](https://x.com/swyx/status/2047378670986342685); cracked devs [on X](https://x.com/beffjezos/status/2048953444585615733) are abandoning Claude Code and coding on Codex.

And OpenAI [is an inference company now](https://x.com/sama/status/2047386068194852963). Build phenomenal cosmic power; [get everything that goes with it](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2SNpkd6p9CU). Round and round, round after round.

The implication, I suppose, is that [this old post](https://benn.substack.com/p/do-ai-companies-work) was partially right: AI *labs* are bad businesses. Models really are fast-evaporating moats, clever employees really are [two weeks](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kaikaushik_lol-boris-cherny-and-cat-wu-are-back-at-activity-7351545813728219136-2ER_/) from leaving, and every billion-dollar model is one competitive release away from being leapfrogged into near-immediate obsolescence. But, *this* comparison was wrong:

> There is, however, one enormous difference [between AI labs and cloud providers like AWS]: *You can’t build a cloud vendor overnight.* Azure doesn’t have to worry about a few executives leaving and building a worldwide network of data centers in 18 months. AWS is an internet business, but it dug its competitive moat in the physical world. …
> What, then, is an LLM vendor’s moat? Brand? Inertia? A better set of applications built on top of their core models? An ever-growing bonfire of cash that keeps its models [a nose ahead](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/bHKPQGHSqqE) of a hundred competitors?

The problem is the premise. AI *companies* are actually no different than cloud providers: They build models, but they *sell *computers. And their lasting moat—their *only *lasting* *moat—is their computers. Models converge; talent migrates. Computers endure.

The other implication—the more useful one, maybe—is that AI whiplash is structural. The [vertigo](https://benn.substack.com/p/take-off) is systemic. The hype cycle is scripted theater. In [science fiction stories](https://benn.substack.com/i/184785771/takeoff), the AI company that builds the best model has a compounding advantage over its competitors. Someone builds the smartest model; that model helps its developers improve itself faster than the second-smartest model; a small gap becomes a big gap; a big gap becomes an insurmountable gap. There is urgency in small differences, because you never know when pulling ahead could explode into pulling away.

Perhaps that is backwards. Perhaps the AI race is less of a space race and more [of a bike race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloton): The headwinds are borne by whoever is in the front. Perhaps it’s [Mario Kart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Kart#:~:text=The%20game%20selects,Shells%20or%20Bananas.): There are hardcoded advantages to being behind. Perhaps there is an [invisible asymptote](https://www.eugenewei.com/blog/2018/5/21/invisible-asymptotes) to every AI company’s lead: The further ahead your AI gets, the more people want to buy it, and the less new AI you can build. Perhaps it’s not a race at all, but a ___ run.[^3] 

Spectating from the [dizzying center of the arena](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wgHnEXl_zug), maybe there is something steadying in that. When the peloton moves together, does it matter who’s winning right now? When no lead is safe, should we bother worrying about the leaderboard?
