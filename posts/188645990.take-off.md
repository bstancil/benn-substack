For a generation, politicians and political scientists have been saying, “[it’s the economy, stupid.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It%27s_the_economy,_stupid)” But *what* is the economy? The traditional answer is that it’s the statistics. It’s the official measures of inflation, unemployment, and other various facts and figures. But a newer answer, popularized by Kyla Scanton,[^1] is that it’s [the vibes](https://kyla.substack.com/p/the-vibecession-the-self-fulfilling). If people think the economy is bad—because the news tells them that, or because their Twitter feeds are full of people talking about how bad it is—they will believe it’s bad. A recession is the statistics. A vibecession is how we feel, and how we feel comes from what we read on Twitter, and what we watch on TikTok.

Just look at us—we can’t stop talking about it. It’s no longer just the grifters, selling us their newsletters and ten hacks for getting the most out of Claude. It’s no longer just the rubes, who’ve seen [the Illuminati](https://x.com/GeoffLewisOrg/status/1945864963374887401) inside of ChatGPT, or a [new physics](https://futurism.com/former-ceo-uber-ai) inside of Grok. It’s all of us, all of the time. We talk about what’s coming next; about how everyone else is wrong about what’s next; about how they aren’t going to make it; about how *we* might not make it.  Overheard, moments ago, from a leader at a non-profit: “I’m convinced that, a year or two from now, most jobs could be managing agents.” And then: “I don’t like it, but, should we give a role to AI?” Or, from earlier this week, at a coffee shop, where I ran into an old coworker: “What do you make of this new agentic analysis tool?,” he asked. Another old coworker walked in. “I think AI has taste,” he said.[^2] The most upvoted story of all time on Hacker News—Silicon Valley’s voting machine; the barometer of its fixations—is the BBC’s announcement that Stephen Hawking died; people [left 450 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=16582136). There are [1,049 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46902223) about Opus 4.6; [1,224 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47050488) about Sonnet 4.6; [623 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46902638) about GPT 5.3 Codex; [864](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47074735) about Gemini 3.1 Pro. There were [511 comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46037637) about Opus 4.5; [796](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45415962) about Sonnet 4.5; [1,066](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45967211) about Gemini 3; [583](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46301851) about Gemini 3 Flash; [1,105](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46234788) about GPT 5.2; [742](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45904551) about GPT 5.1; [2,541](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44826997) about GPT 5. And next month, when a new turn of models [obsoletes](https://www.fastcompany.com/91494576/new-ai-models-are-losing-their-edge-almost-immediately) these, we’ll do it all again.

They say the internet is [dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Internet_theory), full of robots talking to one another. On the contrary—it is furiously, psychotically alive. It is a vortex of this new psychosis, tightening around a single axel, [spinning faster and faster as it does](https://www.instagram.com/reels/C9TD50PxMws/). Log on, and that is all there is.

People sometimes talk about [takeoff](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/ai-takeoff): That moment when AI becomes capable enough to improve itself; when the curve becomes exponential; when the computers take control. It’s often a technical term, about code and algorithmic methods of machine learning. But there is also a social takeoff—when the world *feels* like it’s pinning us against our seats; when the machines conquer our attention; when we begin to believe, rightly or wrongly, in their blistering power; when the rattling starts. 

[Vertigo](https://www.instagram.com/p/CwNpdRlIXCN/?hl=en) is the *sensation* of spinning. It is feeling dizzy, even when the world is standing still. If every Anthropic press release is all we talk about, have the robots not already taken over? If every company is urgently rearranging itself around a workforce of agents, does it matter how well they score on the tests? Are we learning, by posting to our Substacks and reading the discourse, or are we becoming obsessed? Is this takeoff, or just takes? 

[Feel the AGI](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/2026/02/do-you-feel-agi-yet/685845/). But is that the AI, or is it us?
